§01
Who I am

I started as a teacher. I never stopped listening.

Seven years teaching languages, to three-year-olds and to engineers at Thales. Then Amman: first the French Embassy, then running the Jordan programme of a coding bootcamp that trained refugees, Syrian, Yemeni, Iraqi and Palestinian, to become software engineers.

Kuala Lumpur, Singapore, Simplon in Paris, then tech: Algolia, Alan, Decathlon. Different rooms, one thread. Understand where someone really is before deciding where to take them. That was teaching. It turned out to be research.

§02
What I care about
(a)

Rigour and heart, in the same room.

I love rigour, and I’m user-centred to the core. What I need most is a team where everyone is obsessed with quality. I’m happy to be the one obsessed with the user experience, even if it means noticing things users didn’t know about themselves.

(b)

The user, in rooms they aren’t invited to enough.

Roadmap meetings, design reviews, sprint planning: that’s where decisions about users get made, usually without them. I bring them into the room, with a quote, a clip, a moment from the field, until their voice changes the decision.

(c)

Together, or not at all.

Research only has value if we do it together. I can’t see myself disappearing for a month and coming back to deliver a report.

§03
What I do
Upstream

Frame the real problem

Mostly generative research: mapping the problem space before the roadmap fills it in for us.

Methods

Mixed, on purpose

Interviews, diary studies, field shadowing, surveys, usability tests, card sorting, JTBD, segmentation and archetypes, workshops.

Scale

Systems, then agents

Research ops, atomic repositories, templates, rituals, and AI agents so teams can self-serve insight.

§05
Working with me

I'll tell you the uncomfortable finding.

Kindly, clearly, with the quote that proves it.

Light tone, serious substance.

Expect emojis in my docs. And at least one pun per meeting. You've been warned.

I work with you, not next to you.

A hard problem doesn’t scare me. It wakes me up.

I don’t hand over reports.

I share insights once they’re worth acting on, and we build the understanding and the decisions together.

I’m the colleague who asks the uncomfortable questions, so you can make the comfortable decision.
