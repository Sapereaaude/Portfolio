§01
Who I am

I started as a teacher. I never stopped listening.

Seven years teaching languages, to three-year-olds and to engineers at Thales. Then Amman: first the French Embassy, then running the Jordan programme of a coding bootcamp that trained refugees, Syrian, Yemeni, Iraqi and Palestinian, to become software engineers.

Kuala Lumpur, Singapore, Simplon in Paris, then tech: Algolia, Alan, Decathlon. Different rooms, one thread. Understand where someone really is before deciding where to take them. That was teaching. It turned out to be research.

§02
What I care about
(a)

Rigour and heart, in the same room.

Mission-driven places often forgive sloppiness. Rigorous places are often rigorous about something hollow. I'm looking for the overlap, and I try to be it.

(b)

The user, in rooms they weren't invited to.

Research is worth nothing if it stays in a deck. I want findings that change a decision, and I check whether they did.

(c)

Home, and the people losing it.

Displacement, fairness, who pays the price of "efficiency". It runs through my research, my photography and the work I'm proudest of.

§03
What I do
Upstream

Frame the real problem

Mostly generative research: mapping the problem space before the roadmap fills it in for us.

Methods

Mixed, on purpose

Scale

Systems, then agents

Research ops, atomic repositories, templates, rituals, and AI agents so teams can self-serve insight.

§05
Working with me
User manual · v1

I listen, then I systematise.

Empathy and logic first. When I see leverage, I turn it into a method, a template or a ritual.

Written first.

Context, scope, goal, questions. Then we meet. Of course we meet.

I'll tell you the uncomfortable finding.

Kindly, clearly, with the quote that proves it.

Light tone, serious substance.

Expect emojis in my docs. And at least one pun per meeting. You've been warned.

I'm the colleague who reopens the conversation with "but what does the user actually do?", and who watches how you use the coffee machine. Just because.
